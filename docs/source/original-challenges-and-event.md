## Full problem statements

Six core challenges, one bonus challenge, and one open track are published. Tidepath Family Alliance and Mangrove Mews Cat Rescue are fictional. Their problems come from a survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

### P1. From sign-up to quarterly report

**Summary:** Tracking sign-ups, attendance, mentor matches and volunteer hours by hand takes 5 to 15 hours of a Tidepath staff member's week, and the quarterly outcome report takes days. Solved means each detail is entered once, no follow-up is missed, and the report is ready in minutes.

Tidepath Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

**Marisol, the program manager:** I run Tidepath's programs, from mentoring to the workshops we give in schools. Every week I copy new sign-ups from our online form into the program spreadsheet and type in attendance from the paper sheets. I track which kid has which mentor, and I add up our volunteers' hours. None of those lists talk to each other. The same mother can be on three of them, spelled three ways, and reminders go out only when I remember to send them. Last month a kid missed three sessions before anyone called home, because the attendance sheets weren't typed in yet. Every quarter, the outcome report for our funders and the board takes me about 3 days of pulling numbers from every list, and they never match the first time. Most weeks this eats 5 to 15 hours of my time, hours I don't spend with kids and their families.

I just want to type each thing once, know which kids need a call this week, and have the quarterly report ready without losing days to it.

**What a fix must respect:**

- Free or low-cost, with nothing that needs a technical person to keep it running.
- Staff aren't technical, so it has to be learnable in minutes.
- Some tools have to stay, because funders or partners require them. Many of them don't connect to anything and only export CSV files (plain spreadsheet files that any spreadsheet program can open).
- Families' details are private. Only the people who need them should see them.

**What solved looks like:** a judge can see these in your demo.

- Each kid, parent, mentor and volunteer is entered once and has one record, however their name is spelled, and a kid's record holds the sign-up, attendance and mentor.
- The program manager knows, the same week, which kids have missed sessions or haven't met their mentor, and the follow-up has already reached the right person.
- The quarterly outcome report comes together in minutes, and its numbers match every list without anyone checking by hand.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, one made-up quarter taken end to end: one record of sign-ups, attendance, mentor matches and volunteer hours, the follow-ups sent, and the quarterly report built from it.
- As a stretch, every form and export Tidepath uses, an agent that drafts the follow-ups and tracks replies, a view for each staff role, and edge cases like a kid who changes schools.

### P2. Duplicate donor records, and donors who quietly stop giving

**Summary:** Tidepath's donor list holds the same people two or three times over, so some donors get the same letter three times, and nobody notices when a regular donor stops giving. Solved means each donor is listed once, and staff learn of a late gift in time to reach out.

Tidepath Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

**Denise, the fundraising coordinator:** I work part time at Tidepath, raising the money that doesn't come from grants. Gifts reach us through the online donation page, event tickets, the gala auction and checks in the mail, and each keeps its own list. Every month I spend about 5 hours downloading those lists, importing them into our donor database and typing in the checks. One donor ends up there as "Rosa Delgado", "Rosa M. Delgado" and "R. Delgado", with two email addresses, so that donor gets our year-end letter three times, and our totals are wrong. Worse, nothing tells me when someone stops giving. A donor who gave every December didn't give last year, and nobody called. When a monthly donor's credit card expires, the gifts just stop, and I find out months later. Every gift we lose that way is money that never reaches kids and families.

I just want to know each donor is on our list once, and to hear about a late gift while there's still time to call.

**What a fix must respect:**

- No record is merged or deleted until a staff member approves it, and no giving history is ever lost.
- It works from downloaded spreadsheets, since some donation tools can't connect to anything.
- Donor information is private.
- Low-cost, and simple enough for a part-time staff member to run every week.

**What solved looks like:** a judge can see these in your demo.

- Each donor appears once, and no two records are merged until staff have seen why they seem to match and approved the merge.
- Staff know each week which regular donors' gifts are late, with each donor's last gift, usual amount and usual timing, and a personal note suggested for each.
- A failed monthly gift is noticed within days, not months.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, a made-up donor list and its gift history taken end to end: duplicates merged once staff approve, no giving history lost, and every late regular gift found in time to reach out.
- As a stretch, new gifts from every source each week, an agent that drafts a note to each late donor and tracks who was called, and failed monthly gifts caught within days.

### P3. From request to approved workshop

**Summary:** Each workshop request costs Tidepath's front-desk coordinator days of calls, emails and texts: a planning call, a date, slides fitted to the class, the school's sign-off. Solved means every request becomes a confirmed workshop with approved slides, without staff chasing anyone.

Tidepath Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

**Yesenia, the front-desk coordinator:** I'm the first person a school reaches when it wants one of Tidepath's safety or mental health workshops. Requests come to me by phone, email, text and our website's form, and every one starts the same chain. I write back for the date, the room, the language, the topic, and whether it's in person or online. Then I set up a planning call with our program manager, which takes several emails just to find a time. After the call, the program manager fits our slides to that class, and I send them to the school for approval. Last month, two people at one school asked for changes in separate email threads, and we couldn't tell which version they'd approved. The day before each workshop, I call to confirm the room. One workshop for 30 eighth graders took 3 days of this, and every hour I spend chasing is an hour families wait at the front desk.

I just want every request to turn into a confirmed workshop, with slides the school has approved, without me chasing anyone.

**What a fix must respect:**

- Schools and partners won't download an app. It has to work by text, email, phone and the website.
- Requests come in, and workshops are given, in English, Spanish and Haitian Creole.
- Staff check every set of slides before a school sees it.
- It has to use the staff's existing calendars, not a new one.
- A person can step in at any point.
- It has to be low-cost.

**What solved looks like:** a judge can see these in your demo.

- A request that arrives by email, text, phone or the website is answered the same day, asking every missing question at once, and the planning call is on the staff's calendars.
- The school gets slides already fitted to its class's age, topic, language and format, and staff always know which version it signed off on.
- The front-desk coordinator knows, any day, where every request stands, from first message to workshop day, and confirmations and reminders go out without anyone chasing.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, one made-up request taken end to end: from its first message to a confirmed workshop, with slides the school has approved.
- As a stretch, every channel and all three languages, an agent that fits the slides to each class and tracks the school's changes, and edge cases like a school that moves the date.

### P4. Private counseling notes typed twice

**Summary:** Tidepath's counselors write each session note, then type much of it again into a funder's separate system, and all that typing means they see fewer families. Solved means each note is written once, and the funder gets only the details it requires.

Tidepath Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

**Reginald, a counselor:** I'm one of Tidepath's counselors, and most days I see five families: parents and kids working through something hard at home. After each session I write my note in our secure records system, which takes about 20 minutes if I do it right. Then I open one funder's website and type much of the same note again, another 15 minutes, because that funder pays for these sessions and wants its own copy in its own boxes. On a full day, notes take me close to 3 hours, and I finish most of them after the last family has gone home. Every second copy is another chance for a mistake, and a private detail the funder never asked for can land in the wrong box. Every hour I spend typing twice is an hour I can't give to another family.

I just want to write each note once, and know the funder sees only what it needs.

**What a fix must respect:**

- Counseling notes are protected by privacy law, such as HIPAA, the US law that protects health information. They must stay in approved, secure systems.
- Each person sees only what they're allowed to see, and there's a record of who opened what.
- The funder gets only the details it requires, and nothing more.
- Notes never go into public AI chat tools.
- For this hackathon, use only made-up notes, never real ones.

**What solved looks like:** a judge can see these in your demo.

- A counselor writes one note, and the funder's fields fill in from it for the counselor to check.
- The screen shows exactly which details will go to the funder, and nothing else leaves the secure system.
- A record of what was sent, and of who opened each note.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, one made-up week of sessions end to end: each note written once, the funder's version filled from it with only the details that funder requires, and the counselor seeing exactly what will be sent before it goes.
- As a stretch, more than one funder, control over who sees what with a record of who opened each note, and edge cases like a session the funder doesn't pay for.

### P5. Volunteer updates lost in group chats

**Summary:** Tidepath's volunteers report hours, visits and urgent needs in group chats and texts, and the coordinator spends several hours a week copying them by hand. Solved means accurate volunteer hours, and urgent needs that reach the right staff member the same day.

Tidepath Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

**Owen, the volunteer coordinator:** I work part time at Tidepath, keeping track of our 40 volunteers: mentors, event helpers and the drivers who drop off supplies. Nobody fills in a form. They text me, post in our WhatsApp groups and send photos. One mentor writes that she met a kid for an hour and he needs a laptop for school. A driver mentions in passing that a family is out of diapers. Every week I spend about 4 hours scrolling back through hundreds of messages and copying hours and visits into the volunteer spreadsheet. Some get counted twice, some never get counted, and the totals our funders and board see are close to a guess. Worse, an urgent need can sit in a chat for days before anyone acts on it, and a family is waiting the whole time.

I just want every hour counted once, and every urgent need in front of the right person the same day.

**What a fix must respect:**

- Volunteers won't install a new app or learn new software. Reporting has to take a minute on a phone, by text or a short form.
- Some volunteers aren't comfortable with technology.
- Information about kids is private. Share only what's needed, and only with the people who need it.
- Free, or close to it.
- Don't automate WhatsApp to pull messages out of a live chat. It breaks easily, and WhatsApp's terms forbid using automated means to access its service, or to collect information about its users, in ways WhatsApp hasn't authorized. Other ways work: a bot that volunteers message (on WhatsApp, only in a way WhatsApp authorizes), a phone-friendly form, or a chat saved with WhatsApp's Export chat option.

**What solved looks like:** a judge can see these in your demo.

- A volunteer's short message or form, sent from a phone, becomes a logged entry: the hours, who they met, what they saw and any urgent need, with any photos attached.
- An urgent need reaches the right staff member the same day.
- A weekly summary for the coordinator, with volunteer hours ready for funder reports and nothing counted twice.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, one made-up week of chats, texts and photos end to end: every hour and visit counted once in the volunteer record, each urgent need sent to the right staff member the same day, and the totals the board sees.
- As a stretch, a bot or phone form volunteers use directly, an agent that follows up on needs nobody has answered, and edge cases like two volunteers reporting the same visit.

### P6. Grant applications and reports, each with its own rules

**Summary:** Tidepath's director and bookkeeper lose days to grant work, because every funder has its own questions, word limits, spending rules and year. Solved means applications and reports drafted only from Tidepath's own facts, and every grant spent the way its funder requires.

Tidepath Family Alliance is a made-up Miami-Dade nonprofit that helps kids and their families through mentoring, family counseling, and safety and mental health workshops in schools. Its problems are real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

**Claudette, the director:** I lead Tidepath. Most of our money comes from about a dozen grants, and I write every application and report with our program manager and part-time bookkeeper. Each funder asks its own questions, with its own word limits: 250 words for one, 1,000 for the next. Rewriting the same program description takes me about half a day each time. Each grant splits its money into categories, and funders' years start in July, October or January, while QuickBooks only knows ours. Every three months, our bookkeeper rebuilds each grant's spending in a spreadsheet, about 2 hours a grant. One spending mistake could mean paying a funder back. An AI chat tool can give me a first draft, but it makes up facts and sounds like a machine, so checking it takes longer than writing from scratch. In deadline weeks, grant work takes most of our time, time that should go to kids and families.

I just want every application to sound like us and say only what's true, and every grant spent the way its funder requires.

**What a fix must respect:**

- Every fact, number and story must come from Tidepath's own records and show where it came from. Funders check.
- The writing has to sound like a person at Tidepath wrote it.
- Staff read and approve everything before it goes out.
- QuickBooks stays.
- Many funders take applications only on their own websites, so a person still submits each one.
- Low-cost, and easy for staff who aren't technical.

**What solved looks like:** a judge can see these in your demo.

- A draft answer to a funder's question, within its word limit, built only from facts staff have approved. Each fact shows its source, and anything missing is flagged instead of filled in.
- The bookkeeper knows, without rebuilding a spreadsheet, what's been spent and what's left in each of a grant's categories, and the date it must be spent by.
- Each report's numbers match that funder's own dates, without anyone rebuilding them by hand.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, one made-up grant taken end to end: its whole application drafted from approved facts within each word limit, and its spending checked by category in the funder's own year.
- As a stretch, every grant at once, an agent that drafts each report when it's due and tracks every deadline, and edge cases like a category about to run over.

### P7. Bonus challenge: Cat colony updates scattered across chats and photos

**Summary:** It hurts Mangrove Mews' founder, volunteers and cats. Sorting each colony's updates out of WhatsApp messages, photos and shared locations takes hours every week, and urgent cases get missed. Solved means anyone at the rescue sees each colony's state and needs the same day.

Mangrove Mews Cat Rescue is made up. Its problem is real, from our survey of 14 South Florida nonprofits.

**Beatriz, founder of Mangrove Mews:** I started Mangrove Mews six years ago and run it after my day job, with 20 volunteers and feeders. We care for 18 street cat colonies across Miami. Every day my phone fills up: a feeder texts about a new gray cat behind the laundromat, a volunteer sends photos of a limping calico, someone shares a location for a box of kittens. It all has to be recorded: which cats are fixed, which still need TNR (trap, neuter, return), who's sick, and who feeds each colony.

Instead it lives in group chats, my camera roll, a notebook and a spreadsheet only I understand. Two volunteers photograph the same cat, and we count her twice. A pregnant cat's photo sinks under 200 messages, and she gives birth before we can trap her. Sorting it all takes me hours every week, and when a sponsor asks how many cats we've fixed, I can only guess.

I just want anyone on my team to know, the same day, how each colony is doing and what it needs.

**What a fix must respect:**

- Free, or close to it. Mangrove Mews has no budget for software and no office staff, so it has to be simple enough to run without technical help.
- Volunteers and feeders report from their phones, out on the street, in a minute or two.
- Colony locations, and feeders' names and phone numbers, are seen only by the rescue's own people.
- A person at the rescue confirms before two cats' records are merged, or before a cat is marked adopted or missing.
- Don't automate WhatsApp to pull messages out of a live chat: WhatsApp's terms forbid automated access in ways it hasn't authorized. A chat saved with WhatsApp's Export chat option works, and so do messages volunteers send on purpose.
- For this hackathon, use only made-up messages, photos and locations, never real ones.

**What solved looks like:** a judge can see these in your demo.

- A feeder's message, photo and shared location, sent from a phone, update the right colony and the right cat, with nobody retyping them.
- Each colony's current state is clear: its number of cats, how many are fixed and how many are still waiting for TNR, which ones need a vet, and who fed them last and when.
- The same cat, photographed by two volunteers, is counted once.
- Urgent cases stand out the same day: a pregnant or injured cat, new kittens, or a colony that has lost its feeder.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, turn a made-up batch of chat messages, photos, shared locations and volunteer notes into a census of each colony and each cat, working end to end.
- As a stretch, an agent that keeps the census current as new messages arrive, plus reports the rescue can hand to sponsors or the city.
- Features that might help: a map of the colonies, a profile for each cat with its photo and history, and a log of each feeder's visits.

### P8. Open track: your own nonprofit problem

**Summary:** Solve a real problem that a nonprofit has, one you bring yourself. Open your submission with your problem statement: who has the problem, and what it costs them today. Judges score that statement first.

**Who it hurts:** a nonprofit's staff, its volunteers or the people it serves. You choose who, and your problem statement names them. The problem must be real, and it must be a nonprofit's.

**What happens today:** you tell the judges, in your problem statement. Open your submission with it: who has the problem, and what it costs them today, such as hours of staff time, money, or people left waiting for help. A statement that only describes what you built is not a problem statement.

**What a fix must respect:**

- Like every project at this hackathon, yours uses Codex, the OpenAI API or the OpenAI Agents SDK.
- The nonprofit's own limits, such as its budget, its staff's time and skills, and the privacy of the people it serves.
- For this hackathon, use only made-up details about people, never real ones.

**What solved looks like:** judges score your problem statement first. Is the problem real? Is it a nonprofit's problem? Does it say who has the problem and what it costs them today? Then they score how well your project fits it. It helps to end your statement with two or three things a judge can see in your demo once the problem is solved.

**Ideas, not requirements:**

- At minimum, fix the one step that costs the nonprofit the most.
- As a stretch, fix the whole job around it.

**An example of a problem statement told as a story. Your team brings its own.**

**Lourdes, the part-time coordinator at Coralwick Food Pantry:** Every Saturday we give groceries to about 120 families. Our volunteers check each family in on paper, so the line backs up, and some families wait an hour before they get their food. On Monday I spend 4 hours typing those sheets into the county's report, and when a page goes missing, those families drop out of our numbers.

I just want every family checked in within a minute, and the county's report ready the same day.
